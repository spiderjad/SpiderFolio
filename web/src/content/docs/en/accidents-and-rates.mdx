## Report an accident or incident

Anyone in the company can report an **accident**, an **incident** or a **near miss**:

- with **Report an accident**, under **Mine** in the Health & safety menu or on the Summary;
- or by scanning the form's **QR code**, posted for example on the line or in the canteen.

It opens inside Health & safety, with today's date and the time now already filled in, and once sent it goes
back to where you were. They fill in the **Accident or incident** form, which the area creates in its space:
date and time, place, person affected, type, severity, what happened, causes, photos and witnesses. **Don't
write medical data or diagnoses.**

That form's fixed fields can't be removed nor change their type or options (in the editor they show **Fixed
field**); their text can change and you can add your own fields.

## Who sees the cases

An accident is **health data**. Cases are only seen by whoever has **Health data: accidents and fitness for
work** (by default, only the owner; see [Roles and permissions](/en/docs/roles-and-permissions)). Whoever
reported a case sees what they reported.

Each new case gives a notice to whoever has that capability, in the bell and by email. The email doesn't say
what happened nor to whom: only who reported it and the link to the case.

## Investigate a case

Under **Accidents** you filter cases by dates, type and status, and export them to CSV. Opening a case:

- **See what was reported** opens the form's record.
- **Add measure**: a corrective measure with a **Responsible** and a date (**By**). The responsible gets a
  notice 7 days before, the day before and on the day, and sees it in **My measures**, where they mark it done
  (without seeing the case's details). The measure also goes into the [planning](/en/docs/preventive-planning).
- **Move to investigation** while the causes are looked into: the case is **Under investigation** (still among
  the open ones, and it can be filtered on its own); **Back to open** undoes it.
- **Close the case** once every measure is done; it can be **Reopened**.

## Accident rates

The **Rates** section (for whoever manages the area or has **Health data: accidents and fitness for work**)
calculates the rates with the definitions of Spain's INSST:

| Rate               | Calculation                                        |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Frequency rate** | accidents with leave × 1,000,000 / hours worked    |
| **Severity rate**  | days of leave × 1,000 / hours worked               |
| **Incidence rate** | accidents with leave × 100,000 / average headcount |

In Latin America each country may define them differently: the screen says so.

It shows the year to date and the last 12 months, and a chart by month with the frequency rate and the
accidents with leave. **Export CSV** downloads the year's months and both periods.

### What you need to enter

- **Hours and headcount of each month**: whoever manages the area enters each month's hours worked and average
  headcount, for the whole company or a department. Without them there's no rate: if months are missing, the
  screen says so.
- **Days of leave**: accidents with leave are the cases of type “Accident with sick leave”. On each one,
  whoever has health data enters the **Days of leave** (only the number, no medical data); they count for the
  severity rate.

### By department

Rates by department are only for whoever has health data, because with few accidents a department can point at
a person. Everyone else sees only the company's figures, which say nothing about any case.
