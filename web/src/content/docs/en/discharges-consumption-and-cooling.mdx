## The area's summary

**Environment › Summary** shows what's due in the next 60 days (permits, obligations, waste managers'
authorizations, waste maximum storage time and leak checks), the analyses out of limits in the last six months
and last month's consumption against the same month the year before.

## Discharges

It starts from a permit of type **Discharge permit** (in Permits).

1. In **Discharges**, **Edit limits** loads each parameter of the permit with its unit and its minimum or
   maximum: COD, BOD5, suspended solids, oils and fats, pH, conductivity, nitrogen, phosphorus…
2. **Record analysis** asks for the sampling date, the sampling point, the laboratory and each parameter's value
   (others can be added).
3. Each value is compared with its limit: out of limits shows in red and whoever manages the module is told.
4. With the **Quality** module on, **Open nonconformity** creates one with the values out of limits; the
   analysis stays linked to it.

The **Trend** chart shows one parameter over time with its limit as a dashed line. How often analyses are done
is kept as a recurring obligation.

## Consumption and indicators

1. **New meter**: mains or well water, electricity, natural gas, diesel, LPG or other, with its unit (m³, kWh,
   L, kg).
2. Each month, tap the month's cell and type the **meter reading at the end of the month**. The app works out
   the consumption from the previous month's reading. For the first reading or when the meter was replaced,
   type the month's consumption by hand.
3. In **Production (t)**, type the tonnes produced each month: they give the **ratio per tonne** (m³/t,
   kWh/t).
4. **Target per t** sets the year's goal for each meter; the months and the total above it show in red.

Each meter has its chart with the year's consumption and the previous year's.

## Cooling units and fluorinated gases

**New unit** asks for the name, the location, the **refrigerant** (R-404A, R-449A, R-744…), the **charge in
kg** and whether it has a **leak detection system**. The global warming potential (GWP) comes from a reference
table; for a refrigerant that isn't there, type it.

The app works out:

- The **tonnes of CO₂ equivalent** (charge × GWP / 1000).
- The **leak check period** of Regulation (EU) 2024/573: 5 to 50 t, every 12 months; 50 to 500, every 6; from
  500, every 3; with leak detection, twice as long. Under 5 t there's no mandatory check.
- A warning if the refrigerant has a GWP of 2500 or more with 40 t or more, for the servicing restrictions.

Ammonia (R-717) and CO₂ (R-744) aren't fluorinated gases: their inspections are kept as a recurring obligation.

**Log** records on the unit a leak check (and whether a leak was found), a recharge, a recovery, a repair or
an inspection, with the kilos, the company and the certificate. Each leak check moves the date of the next
one, which gives notice before it's due.
