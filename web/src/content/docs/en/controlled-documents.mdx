## What they're for

A **controlled document** is a page or a file with a **code** (for example, PRC-004), **versions**, **approval** and **a single current version**. It's what quality standards ask for: everyone reads the approved version, changes go through review and there's a record of who did what.

<Shot name="documento-controlado" alt="A controlled page with its code and status next to the title" />

## Convert a document

You need the **Manage** level on the document. Folders can't be converted.

1. Open **Document control**: on a page, from **More actions** (the three dots); on a file, from its menu in the explorer.
2. Choose the **Document type**:
   - **MAN** (Manual): describes the whole system.
   - **POL** (Policy): commitments and general rules.
   - **PRC** (Procedure): what is done, by whom and in what order.
   - **INS** (Work instruction): how to do a task step by step.
   - **FOR** (Form): template that is filled in as a record.
3. Choose the **Responsible department**. If you don't choose one, the space's department is used. It approves the document by default.
4. The dialog shows the code that will be assigned. Click **Convert to controlled document**.

The current version becomes **draft 1**. To make it current, send it for review (see [Approvals](/en/docs/approvals)).

## Codes

The code is built from the type's prefix and a number that goes up on its own: PRC-001, PRC-002… Each type also sets how many months apart it's reviewed, which gives each document its **Next review** date.

Types, prefixes and numbering are configured in **Administration** › **Controlled documents** › **Numbering**. Changing the prefix or the padding only affects new codes, and the counter can only go up (lowering it would repeat codes already in use).

## Statuses

Each version goes through these statuses:

1. **Draft**: it's being written and edited.
2. **In review**: it was sent to the approvers. The content is frozen.
3. **Approved**: everyone approved; it's ready to publish.
4. **Published**: it's the **current version**. There's only one.
5. **Obsolete**: the version that was current until a new one was published. It isn't deleted.

A **Rejected** version is discarded and stays in the history without being published.

## The current version

- People who can only view the document always read the current version, even while changes are in progress. A notice tells them the code and version they're viewing.
- People who edit work on the draft. Editing a published document opens a new draft version; the current one doesn't change until the new one is published.
- Bian and QR codes always use the current version.

## The control dialog

**Document control** brings together everything about the document:

<Shot name="control" alt="The control dialog with the current version, the responsible department, the actions and the version history" />

- **Current version**, **Responsible** and **Next review**.
- The actions available to you: **Send for review**, **Approve**, **Request changes**, **Reject**, **Publish** or **Withdraw from review**.
- **Compare versions**, if there's more than one.
- **Version history**: each version with its status, who sent it, who approved it, the reason for the change and whether it was a **Minor change** (published without requesting a new reading).
- **Required reading**, once there's a published version (see [Required reading](/en/docs/required-reading)).

## Publish

Whoever can approve the document publishes it, once the version is **Approved**. In **Publish this version?**:

- The version becomes the current one and the previous one becomes obsolete.
- **Request a new reading from everyone who had to read the previous version** is checked. If you uncheck it, it counts as a minor change and the previous readings remain valid.
- **Reason for the change**: one line for those who need to reread it.

## Compare versions

**Compare versions** shows additions in green and removals in red strikethrough. Choose the **Starting version** (by default, the current one or the previous one) and the **Version to compare** (by default, the newest one), and check **Changes only** to hide what didn't change. For pages the text is compared; for files, for now, only the details of each version.

## Controlled PDF

In the **Version history**, every published or obsolete version has a button to download its controlled PDF:

- It starts with a **control sheet**: company, code, title, version, effective date, who prepared it and who approved it.
- Every page carries the footer "Uncontrolled copy · downloaded by … on …", with your name and the date. Obsolete versions also carry "OBSOLETE" diagonally.
- For PDF files, the control sheet is added to the original. Pages are converted to PDF with Gotenberg; if your installation doesn't have it, you'll see a notice (see [Gotenberg](/en/docs/gotenberg)).

## The document list

People who approve documents, request readings or lead an area have **Administration** › **Controlled documents**: the list with code, current version, status, department and next review, with filters and search, plus the **Approvals**, **Compliance** and **Numbering** tabs. A head only sees their area's documents.
