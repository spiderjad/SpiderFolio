## What it's for

**HACCP** is the base of IFS and BRCGS: the hazards of each process step and the **critical control points**
(CCPs) where they're controlled, with their limit, how they're monitored, what's done if the limit is exceeded
and how it's verified. In SpiderFolio:

- the **plan** is a [controlled document](/en/docs/controlled-documents) of type **HACCP plan**, with its
  version in force, its approval and its yearly review;
- the **CCP table** is a list in the Quality area, each CCP linked to its monitoring form;
- **monitoring** is done with [forms](/en/docs/forms) with a minimum and maximum: a value out of limits opens
  its [nonconformity](/en/docs/nonconformities#from-a-form-record) by itself.

It needs the [Quality module](/en/docs/quality) on.

## The HACCP plan

Turning the module on creates the **HACCP plan** document type (reviewed every 12 months). Upload your plan
(flow diagrams, hazard analysis) as a controlled document of that type and approve it with the HACCP team. In
**HACCP plan**, at the top, the document in force shows with a link.

## The CCP table

In **HACCP plan** (Quality menu) each CCP shows:

- its **code** (PCC-1), name, process step and hazard type (biological, chemical, physical or allergen);
- the **hazard** and the **critical limit**;
- the monitoring **frequency** and its responsible person;
- the **corrective action** if the limit is exceeded and the **verification**;
- the **last 30 days** of its form: how many records, how many out of limits and the open nonconformities;
- **Records**, which opens its form, and **QR**, which opens its settings with the QR code to post on the
  line: scanning it fills in a record.

Whoever manages quality adds one with **New CCP**, edits it, orders it with the arrows and **archives** it
when it no longer applies (it isn't deleted; **Show archived** lists them). Whoever has **View quality**
reads it.

## The meat template

In **Administration** › **Security** › **General**, on the **Food quality** card, **Load the meat template**
creates the **Monitoring records (CCP)** folder in the Quality space with six forms and their six CCPs:

| CCP   | Form                   | Example critical limit                      | NC severity |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| PCC-1 | Raw material reception | Fresh meat ≤ 7 °C; correct condition        | Major       |
| PCC-2 | Cold room temperature  | 0 to 4 °C                                   | Major       |
| PCC-3 | Drying curve           | aw ≤ 0.92 and pH ≤ 5.3 at exit              | Major       |
| PCC-4 | Cooking                | ≥ 72 °C at the product's core               | Critical    |
| PCC-5 | Cooling                | ≤ 4 °C within 6 hours                       | Major       |
| PCC-6 | Metal detector         | All three test pieces detected and rejected | Critical    |

Each form opens its nonconformity by itself when a record is out of limits. The limits are common examples of
the industry: adjust them to your plan in each form's design and in its CCP. How often and who fills them in
are chosen in each form's settings. It never overwrites what you already have with the same name.

## Product on hold

When product is affected, it's **put on hold** until someone decides what to do with it:

- **From a nonconformity**: on its record, **Product on hold** › **Put product on hold** (whoever handles
  it).
- **Alone**: in **Product on hold**, **Put product on hold** (whoever manages quality).

You note the product, the lot, the quantity, where it is and why it's on hold. It's **On hold**.

<Shot
  name="calidad-retenido"

**Deciding** is for whoever manages quality: choose **Released**, **Reworked**, **Diverted** or **Destroyed /
animal by-product** (Regulation 1069/2009), write the grounds and **sign on screen**. The decision keeps who
and when, it's never changed nor deleted, and if the product belongs to a nonconformity it's also in its
history.
